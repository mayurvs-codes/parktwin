# SLIDE 2 — PROBLEM STATEMENT

## **The Parking Intelligence Gap**

### Current Situation

**Existing parking facilities have:**
- CCTV cameras generating continuous video
- Large numbers of vehicles entering and exiting
- Multiple parking spaces and drive lanes
- Limited real-time occupancy visibility
- Manual monitoring and intervention
- No unified representation of the entire facility

### Core Problem

> **How can we transform raw parking-lot video into structured, real-time parking intelligence?**

### Key Questions

* **WHO?** Which vehicle is entering?
* **WHERE?** Where is the vehicle within the facility?
* **WHICH SPACE?** Which parking space is available or occupied?
* **WHAT STATE?** Is the vehicle entering, parked, moving or exiting?
* **WHAT ACTION?** Should the system guide, alert or update the operator?

### What to say:

“The fundamental problem is not the absence of cameras.

Parking facilities already have cameras generating large amounts of visual data.

The problem is that this data is largely passive.

A camera can show us a vehicle, but the parking system needs to understand **which vehicle it is, where it is, which parking space it is associated with, what state that space is in, and what action should follow.**

Therefore, our core problem statement is:

**How can we convert raw parking-lot video into structured, real-time parking intelligence?**

This becomes the foundation for the entire ParkTwin architecture.”

# SLIDE 3 — EXISTING CHALLENGES

## **Why Traditional Parking Monitoring Is Insufficient**

### 01 — Passive CCTV
Cameras provide video, but do not inherently provide structured parking state.

### 02 — Fragmented Information
Vehicle identification, occupancy, navigation and analytics are often isolated.

### 03 — Manual Monitoring
Operators must interpret multiple camera feeds and manually identify events.

### 04 — No Spatial Intelligence
A vehicle detection does not automatically tell the system which physical parking space it affects.

### 05 — Limited Driver Assistance
Drivers may still need to search manually for available or reserved spaces.

### 06 — No Unified Operational Model
There is no single live representation connecting:
$$\mathbf{Vehicle \longrightarrow Camera \longrightarrow Location \longrightarrow Parking\ Space \longrightarrow Event \longrightarrow User}$$

### What to say:

“Once we examined the problem in more detail, we identified several challenges.

First, CCTV is primarily a **passive observation system**.

Second, the information generated by different systems is often fragmented.

Third, operators may have to manually interpret multiple camera feeds.

Another important challenge is spatial understanding.

Detecting a vehicle is not enough.

The system needs to understand the relationship between that vehicle and the actual physical parking facility.

Finally, there is a gap on the driver side.

Even if the operator knows which spaces are available, the driver still needs to find that space.

This led us to the central design principle of ParkTwin:

**connect vehicle intelligence with spatial intelligence.**”

# SLIDE 4 — METHODOLOGY

## **ParkTwin Methodology**
### Perception → Event → Intelligence → Digital Twin → Action

### STEP 1 — CAMERA INGESTION
CCTV / IP Camera / Recorded Video

$$\downarrow$$

### STEP 2 — COMPUTER VISION
* **Vehicle Detection**: YOLOv8
* **Multi-Object Tracking**: ByteTrack
* **ANPR**: PaddleOCR

$$\downarrow$$

### STEP 3 — EVENT GENERATION
Convert visual observations into structured events:
* Vehicle ID
* License Plate
* Camera ID
* Timestamp
* Position
* Movement
* Confidence

### What to say:

“Our methodology begins at the camera layer.

ParkTwin can consume CCTV streams, IP-camera feeds or recorded parking footage.

The first stage is perception.

We use computer vision to detect vehicles and multi-object tracking to maintain vehicle identity across frames.

ANPR is then used to extract license-plate information.

But an important design decision is that we do not treat these detections as the final output.

Instead, we convert them into **structured events**.

An event can contain the vehicle identity, camera, timestamp, position, movement and associated metadata.

This event becomes the basic unit of information throughout the rest of our system.”

# SLIDE 5 — SOLUTION ARCHITECTURE

## **From Events to Parking Intelligence**

### Architecture Pipeline

1. **CAMERAS**: Ingestion of video feeds.
2. **VISION ENGINE**: YOLO + ByteTrack + ANPR.
3. **EVENT / TELEMETRY LAYER**: Real-time structured events over WebSockets/SSE.
4. **PARKING INTELLIGENCE ENGINE**: Vehicle state, parking state, occupancy, reservation, and violation logic.
5. **DIGITAL TWIN**: Facility geometry, parking spaces, cameras, vehicles, and routes.
6. **APPLICATION LAYER**: Operator Dashboard | Driver Dashboard | Analytics.

### What to say:

“Once structured events are generated, they enter the intelligence layer.

This is where ParkTwin moves beyond computer vision.

The Parking Intelligence Engine interprets those events and maintains the operational state of vehicles and parking spaces.

The system then maps this state onto the Digital Twin.

The Digital Twin contains the spatial representation of the facility — parking spaces, drive lanes, cameras and other relevant entities.

Finally, the application layer consumes this information.

This gives us two major interfaces.

The **operator interface**, which provides monitoring and analytics.

And the **driver interface**, which provides parking discovery and navigation.

The important architectural principle is separation of responsibilities.

The vision engine does not need to know how the dashboard works, and the dashboard does not need to process raw video.”

# SLIDE 6 — END-TO-END SOLUTION

## **Vehicle Entry → Parking → Exit**

1. **01 — ENTRY**: Vehicle enters the monitored facility.
2. **02 — IDENTIFICATION**: Camera detects vehicle and ANPR identifies it.
3. **03 — STATE CREATION**: System creates a structured vehicle event.
4. **04 — PARKING DECISION**: Available / reserved parking space is determined.
5. **05 — NAVIGATION**: Digital Twin generates an in-facility route.
6. **06 — PARKING**: Vehicle reaches the assigned space; Digital Twin updates parking state.
7. **07 — MONITORING**: Vehicle remains associated with the parking session.
8. **08 — EXIT**: Vehicle is detected at the exit; parking state and session are closed.

### What to say:

“This slide represents the complete operational flow we are building toward.

A vehicle first enters the facility.

The system detects and identifies it.

That generates a structured event.

The parking intelligence layer then determines the appropriate parking state.

For a reserved vehicle, the system can associate it with the reserved space.

For a walk-in vehicle, it can identify an available space.

The Digital Twin then provides the spatial representation required for navigation.

Once the vehicle reaches the space, the parking state changes to occupied.

The system continues maintaining that vehicle's parking session.

Finally, when the vehicle reaches the exit, the exit event closes the parking session and updates the Digital Twin.

So the objective is an **end-to-end closed-loop parking system**, rather than a collection of independent features.”

# SLIDE 8 — REQUIREMENTS TO COMPLETE THE PROJECT

## **What Is Required for Full-Scale Completion?**

### 01 — CAMERA INFRASTRUCTURE
- RTSP / ONVIF-compatible IP cameras
- Sufficient camera coverage
- Entry and exit coverage
- Parking-bay coverage
- Correct camera calibration

### 02 — COMPUTE INFRASTRUCTURE
- Dedicated GPU inference server / edge device
- Sufficient VRAM
- Real-time video processing capability
- Reliable network connectivity

### 03 — FACILITY DATA
- Accurate architectural floor plan
- Verified parking-space coordinates
- Drive-lane geometry
- Camera locations
- Entry / exit points
- Floor-to-floor mapping for multi-level facilities

### 04 — DATA & AI
- Larger representative CCTV dataset
- Vehicle and license-plate annotations
- Camera-specific calibration
- Accuracy validation under different lighting/weather conditions
- Continuous model evaluation

### 05 — SOFTWARE & INTEGRATION
- Production-grade backend
- Real-time event infrastructure
- Database deployment
- User authentication
- Reservation system integration
- Access-control integration
- Monitoring and logging

### 06 — FIELD VALIDATION
- Real parking facility deployment
- Multiple simultaneous vehicles
- Day/night testing
- Occlusion testing
- Camera failure testing
- End-to-end latency measurement
- Long-duration reliability testing

### What to say:

“To take ParkTwin from a prototype to a fully deployable system, several additional requirements are necessary.

First is **physical infrastructure** — appropriate camera coverage at entry, exit and parking areas.

Second is compute infrastructure capable of processing multiple video streams in real time.

Third is accurate facility information.

The Digital Twin is only as accurate as the underlying geometry, so verified parking-space coordinates, lanes, camera positions and access points are essential.

On the AI side, we need larger and more representative datasets and extensive validation across lighting, camera angles, occlusion and traffic conditions.

Finally, the system needs to be tested in an actual operational parking facility with multiple simultaneous vehicles.

So the prototype demonstrates the technical foundation.

**Full completion requires combining that software foundation with real-world infrastructure, data and field validation.**

That is the path from a working prototype to a deployable smart parking platform.”
